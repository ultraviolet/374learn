For regular language transformation problems, it's important that you fully understand the transformation in question. A good first step is to think about a very simple language and consider what strings are accepted by the transformed language. For any non-empty string $w$ with $|w| \geq 2$, let $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(w)$ denote the string obtained by deleting the second symbol in $w$. We define: $$\mathsf{Insert2nd}(L) = \{ w \in \Sigma^* \mid |w| \geq 2 \text{ and } \mathsf{Delete2nd}(w) \in L \}$$ In other words, $\mathsf{Insert2nd}(L)$ contains all strings $w$ of length at least $2$ such that deleting the second symbol of $w$ yields a string in $L$. For example, with $L = \{0\}$: - $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(00) = 0$, so $00 \in \mathsf{Insert2nd}(\{0\})$ - $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(10) = 0$, so $10 \in \mathsf{Insert2nd}(\{0\})$ - $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(01) = 1 \neq 0$, so $01 \notin \mathsf{Insert2nd}(\{0\})$
Suppose that $L = \{ 10 \}$. What strings are contained in $\mathsf{Insert2nd}(L)$? 100 110 10 01 1000 010 **Explanation:** We need strings $w$ of length $\geq 2$ where $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(w) = 10$. - $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(100) = 10$ ✓ (delete the second $0$: $1 \cdot \underline{0} \cdot 0 \to 10$) - $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(110) = 10$ ✓ (delete the second $1$: $1 \cdot \underline{1} \cdot 0 \to 10$) - $10$: has length $2$, but $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(10) = 1 \neq 10$ ✗ - $01$: $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(01) = 0 \neq 10$ ✗ - $1000$: $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(1000) = 100 \neq 10$ ✗ - $010$: $\mathsf{Delete2nd}(010) = 00 \neq 10$ ✗
