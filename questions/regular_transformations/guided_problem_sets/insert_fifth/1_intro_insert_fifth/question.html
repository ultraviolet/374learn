For regular language transformation problems, it's important that you fully understand the transformation in question. A good first step is to think about a very simple language and consider what strings are accepted by the transformed language. Consider the following language transformation: $$L' = \{xay \mid xy \in L ~\text{for some}~ x,y \in \Sigma^* ~\text{and}~ a \in \Sigma ~\text{such that}~ |x| = 4\}$$ This transformation inserts a character $a$ after exactly 4 characters from the original string.
Suppose that $L = \{ 111111 \}$. What strings are contained in $L'$? 11110111 11111111 1111111 111101111 11111011 01111111 **Explanation:** We need strings that can be formed by inserting a single character (either $0$ or $1$) after exactly 4 characters from the string $111111$. Since the original string is $111111$ (6 characters), we split it as $x = 1111$ (4 characters) and $y = 11$ (remaining 2 characters). Then we insert a character $a$ between them: $xay$. - $11110111$ ✓ (insert $0$ after 4 characters: $1111 \cdot 0 \cdot 11$) - $11111111$ ✓ (insert $1$ after 4 characters: $1111 \cdot 1 \cdot 11$) - $1111111$ ✗ (this is only 7 characters, we need to insert a character, not remove one) - $111101111$ ✗ (this would be inserting at position 5, not after position 4) - $11111011$ ✗ (this inserts $0$ after 5 characters, not 4) - $01111111$ ✗ (this inserts $0$ before the string, at position 0, not after position 4)
