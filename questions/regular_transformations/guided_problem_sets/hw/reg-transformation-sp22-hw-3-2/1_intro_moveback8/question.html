For regular language transformation problems, it's important that you fully understand the transformation in question. A good first step is to think about a very simple language and consider what strings are accepted by the transformed language. Consider the following language transformation: $$L' = \text{MoveBack}_8(L) = \{xayz : xyaz \in L ~\text{for some}~ x, y, z \in \{0, 1\}^{*} ~\text{and}~ a \in \{0, 1\} ~\text{such that}~ |{y}| \leq 8\}$$ In other words, $L'$ is the language obtained by taking strings from $L$ and moving a character backward by at most 8 positions. For example: - If $w = 0100101001\textbf{1}0011$ is in $L$, then $01\textbf{1}001010010011$ is in $L'$ (move the bold $1$ backward by 8 positions) - If $w = 11\textbf{0}11011$ is in $L$, then $1\textbf{0}1111011$ is in $L'$ (move the bold $0$ backward by 2 positions) - If $w = \textbf{1}0101$ is in $L$, then $w$ itself is also in $L'$ (move the bold $1$ backward by 0 positions, i.e., no movement)
Suppose that $L = \{ 0011 \}$. What strings are contained in $L'$? 0011 0101 1001 0110 1010 1100 **Explanation:** We need strings $w'$ such that we can form $0011 \in L$ by moving a character forward by at most 8 positions. The transformation takes $xyaz \in L$ and produces $xayz \in L'$ where $|y| \leq 8$. So we're moving character $a$ backward over string $y$. Given $xyaz = 0011$, we can produce $xayz$ by choosing different decompositions: 1. $x=\varepsilon, y=\varepsilon, a=0, z=011$: gives $xayz = 0011$ ✓ (no movement) 2. $x=\varepsilon, y=0, a=0, z=11$: gives $xayz = 0011$ (duplicate - no net change) 3. $x=\varepsilon, y=00, a=1, z=1$: gives $xayz = 0101$ ✓ (move first $1$ back 2 positions) 4. $x=\varepsilon, y=001, a=1, z=\varepsilon$: gives $xayz = 1001$ ✓ (move last $1$ back 3 positions) 5. $x=0, y=\varepsilon, a=0, z=11$: gives $xayz = 0011$ (duplicate) 6. $x=0, y=0, a=1, z=1$: gives $xayz = 0101$ (duplicate) 7. $x=0, y=01, a=1, z=\varepsilon$: gives $xayz = 1001$ (duplicate) 8. $x=00, y=\varepsilon, a=1, z=1$: gives $xayz = 0011$ (duplicate) 9. $x=00, y=1, a=1, z=\varepsilon$: gives $xayz = 1001$ (duplicate) So the distinct strings in $L'$ are: $0011, 0101, 1001$. - $0110$ is not in $L'$ because we cannot decompose $0011$ to produce it via the transformation - $1010$ is not in $L'$ for similar reasons
