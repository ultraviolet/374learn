For regular language transformation problems, it's important that you fully understand the transformation in question. A good first step is to think about a very simple language and consider what strings are accepted by the transformed language. Consider the following language transformation: We define the language transformation as: $$L' = \{x1yz \mid xy1z \in L\}$$ In other words, if a string in $L$ has a $1$ at some position (specifically, a string of the form $xy1z$), then the string formed by moving that $1$ one position to the right (forming $x1yz$) is in $L'$. For example: - If $0011 \in L$, then $0101 \in L'$ (the $1$ at position 2 moves to position 1) - If $1100 \in L$, then $1010 \in L'$ (the $1$ at position 1 moves to position 2) - If $101 \in L$, then $110 \in L'$ (the $1$ at position 1 moves to position 2) Note: The transformation applies to **one specific occurrence** of $1$ in the string. If there are multiple $1$s, any one of them could potentially be moved.
Suppose that $L = \{ 01, 10 \}$. What strings are contained in $L'$? 10 01 00 11 001 100 010 **Explanation:** We need strings $w = x1yz$ such that $xy1z \in L$. From $01 \in L$: - Let $x = \epsilon, y = \epsilon, z = \epsilon$: Then $xy1z = 01 \in L$ and $x1yz = 10 \in L'$ ✓ From $10 \in L$: - Let $x = \epsilon, y = \epsilon, z = \epsilon$: Then $xy1z = 10 \in L$ and $x1yz = 01 \in L'$ ✓ Note that we can only move a $1$ that already exists in the string, and we can only move it one position to the right. Since $L$ only contains strings of length 2 with exactly one $1$, moving that $1$ one position to the right gives us both possible length-2 strings with one $1$. Strings like $00$, $11$, $001$, etc. cannot be in $L'$ because: - $00$ would require $0\underline{1}0 \in L$ (doesn't exist) - $11$ would require $1\underline{1}1 \in L$ (doesn't exist, and $L$ only has length-2 strings) - $001$ would require $00\underline{1}1 \in L$ or $0\underline{1}01 \in L$ (both don't exist)
