For regular language transformation problems, it's important that you fully understand the transformation in question. A good first step is to think about a very simple language and consider what strings are accepted by the transformed language. Consider the following language transformation: $$L' = \{xy \mid x \cdot 1^n \cdot y \in L ~\text{for some}~ n \ge 0\}$$ In other words, $L'$ consists of all strings that can be formed by taking a string in $L$ and removing some (possibly empty) sequence of consecutive 1s. For example: - If $x1111y \in L$, then $xy \in L'$ (we can remove the four consecutive 1s) - If $x11y \in L$, then $xy \in L'$ (we can remove the two consecutive 1s) - If $xy \in L$, then $xy \in L'$ (we can remove zero 1s, i.e., $n = 0$)
Suppose that $L = \{ 011 \}$. What strings are contained in $L'$? 011 01 0 11 0111 00 **Explanation:** We need strings $w$ such that inserting some number of consecutive 1s into $w$ gives us $011$. - $011 \in L'$ ✓ (insert zero 1s: $011 = 011$) - $01 \in L'$ ✓ (insert one 1 between the 0 and the 1: $0 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 = 011$) - $0 \in L'$ ✓ (insert two 1s after the 0: $0 \cdot 11 \cdot \varepsilon = 011$) - $11 \notin L'$ ✗ (cannot insert consecutive 1s into $11$ to get $011$) - $0111 \notin L'$ ✗ (would require removing a 1, not inserting) - $00 \notin L'$ ✗ (cannot insert consecutive 1s into $00$ to get $011$)
