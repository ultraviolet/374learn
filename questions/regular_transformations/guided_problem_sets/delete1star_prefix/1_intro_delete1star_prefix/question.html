For regular language transformation problems, it's important that you fully understand the transformation in question. A good first step is to think about a very simple language and consider what strings are accepted by the transformed language. Consider the following language transformation: $$L' = \{w \mid 1^n \cdot w \in L ~\text{for some}~ n \ge 0\}$$ In other words, $L'$ consists of all strings that can be formed by taking a string in $L$ and removing a (possibly empty) prefix of consecutive 1s. For example: - If $1111w \in L$, then $w \in L'$ (we can remove the four 1s from the beginning) - If $11w \in L$, then $w \in L'$ (we can remove the two 1s from the beginning) - If $w \in L$, then $w \in L'$ (we can remove zero 1s, i.e., $n = 0$) Note: This transformation only removes 1s from the **beginning** of the string, not from the middle.
Suppose that $L = \{ 1110 \}$. What strings are contained in $L'$? 1110 110 10 0 11110 111 11 1 **Explanation:** We need strings $w$ such that prepending some number of 1s to $w$ gives us $1110$. - $1110 \in L'$ ✓ (prepend zero 1s: $\varepsilon \cdot 1110 = 1110$) - $110 \in L'$ ✓ (prepend one 1: $1 \cdot 110 = 1110$) - $10 \in L'$ ✓ (prepend two 1s: $11 \cdot 10 = 1110$) - $0 \in L'$ ✓ (prepend three 1s: $111 \cdot 0 = 1110$) - $11110 \notin L'$ ✗ (would require removing a 1, not prepending) - $111 \notin L'$ ✗ (cannot prepend 1s to $111$ to get $1110$) - $11 \notin L'$ ✗ (cannot prepend 1s to $11$ to get $1110$) - $1 \notin L'$ ✗ (cannot prepend 1s to $1$ to get $1110$)
